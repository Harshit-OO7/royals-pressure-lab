04 / INSIDE THE METHOD

Make every assumption inspectable.

01

Reconstruct the situation

Scores, wickets and legal balls are measured before each delivery. Public IPL data verifies the supplied records and repairs three missing deliveries. Rain revisions and unresolved exceptions are excluded.

02

Identify pressure

Tight or difficult chases, early collapses and resource-constrained first-innings finishes. Rules use the current state, never the final result. These are testable pressure proxies.

03

Compare like contexts

Expected runs and dismissals use the previous three IPL seasons, grouped by innings, phase, wickets and required-rate band. Sparse groups borrow evidence from phase averages.

04

Test the pressure premium

Overall context-adjusted ability anchors the estimate. Historical validation selects shrinkage and the additional pressure weight. Both disciplines selected zero extra pressure weight. Shared match resamples quantify sampling uncertainty.

Exact pressure rules and rating formula

Tight chase: ≤30 balls remaining, required rate 6–15. Difficult chase: 31–90 balls, required rate 10–16, ≥3 wickets available. Collapse: ≥3 wickets before 36 balls or ≥5 before 90. First-innings finish: ≤24 balls left and ≤5 wickets available.

Delivery value: batting runs above expectation minus 15 times excess dismissals; bowling runs saved plus 15 times excess wickets. Past-season unadjusted venue residuals receive a 600-event prior; 2022 uses no venue correction. Rating = 100 × [overall value / (events + selected prior) + pressure weight × residual pressure adjustment]. The 15-run wicket value remains a simplifying assumption.

2024–25 future pressure errors select the prior and pressure weight. 2026 is a temporal diagnostic, not an untouched confirmatory test after this redesign. Eligibility: 60 pressure events, eight pressure matches, 180 total events and observed participation in 2026. Supplied 2022–2026 IPL performances supply all ranked evidence. Public IPL 2019–2021 data trains the initial 2022 baseline only. Each scored season uses the preceding three IPL seasons. The model has no learned player-opposition adjustment or win-probability leverage model. Ratings are general ability estimates evaluated for pressure use, not proof of clutch skill.

IPL focus and supplied T20I evidence

Primary rankings use IPL performance. Supplied T20I and other T20 records form a sensitivity diagnostic only. An unadjusted cross-competition score is not treated as equivalent to IPL quality. Players without sufficient IPL exposure need a separate transfer model and are outside this research ranking.
